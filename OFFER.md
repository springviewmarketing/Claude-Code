# The Spring View Offer

Locked August 2026. This document is the commercial reasoning behind `index.html`.
The page is the expression. This is the argument.

---

## 1. What changed and why

The previous offer was £450 a month with a £200 refundable first month. It sold, twice.
The problem was never that it was too expensive. The problem was that it was priced like a
content package rather than like a territorial monopoly on the marketing of an entire town's
independent optician.

Three things were being given away for nothing:

1. **Exclusivity.** One practice per catchment area is a genuine opportunity cost. Every
   practice taken is a whole town Tom can never sell into again. That was buried at block 9
   of the old page as a nice-to-have.
2. **The photography.** A UK half day brand shoot with a commercial licence is £430 to £650.
   It was being described as an onboarding step rather than as an asset the practice keeps forever.
3. **The setup work.** The audit, the review scripts, the recall list, the desk scripts. All
   of it was being done anyway, unnamed and unpriced, which means it was worth nothing to the buyer.

The new offer prices the monopoly, names the assets, and stacks the setup work as bonuses.

## 2. The price

| | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| First month | £200 | **£250** |
| Monthly thereafter | £450 | **£750** |
| Minimum term | None | None |
| Notice period | None | None |

**£750 a month.** A 67% increase on £450, and still:

- less than the cheapest content-led social agency retainer (£1,000 to £2,000/mo, where the
  agency creates the content rather than asking you to supply it)
- comfortably under the UK marketing retainer median for 2026 (£1,250 to £3,500/mo)
- under the £1,000 psychological ceiling a single-site practice applies to a monthly commitment
- roughly a third of the value of the same components bought separately (£2,050/mo)

At twenty practices that is £15,000 a month rather than £9,000, against a delivery model the
August playbook already costs at half a day per client per month.

**£250 first month.** Held at roughly a third of the ongoing price, same as before. Its job is
not revenue. Its job is to make saying yes cost less than saying no.

### Why not higher

£950 would still be defensible against the stack, but it crosses the line where a single-site
independent starts routing the decision through their accountant. £750 stays a decision the
owner can make alone, on the call, which is where this offer is designed to close.

### Why the price rise is itself a lever

Anyone previously quoted £450 should be told the price moves to £750 and offered the old rate
if they start before a stated date. That is genuine urgency, honestly earned, and it is the
cheapest way to convert a stalled pipeline. Own the increase; do not apologise for it.

## 3. The value equation

Hormozi: `Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)`

| Lever | How this offer moves it |
|---|---|
| **Dream outcome** | Be the practice the town finds first. Stop losing patients to the chain by default. Stated as the outcome, not as a list of deliverables. |
| **Perceived likelihood** | Named market rates for every component. A real Visibility Report with checkable findings. Weekly GBP updates against a monthly promise. Evidence attached to each bonus (the 11%-in-90-days recall figure, the carousel engagement data). Refusal to promise a patient number, which reads as more credible than promising one. |
| **Time delay** | The Visibility Report lands before month one is out. Review count is the number that moves first and it moves within weeks. Baseline captured on day one so movement is visible from the first report. |
| **Effort and sacrifice** | Under an hour a month. One photography visit. Approve or don't. The practice supplies nothing, least of all photographs, which is the part every competitor pushes back onto the client. |

The denominator is the whole game here. An optician is not short of money. They are short of
Tuesday evenings.

## 5. The bonuses

Hormozi's rule: decompose every problem the prospect will hit after they say yes, then solve
each one and name it. These came out of the Reddit practitioner research, not out of the air.
Each carries the evidence that justifies it.

### 01 The Visibility Report (£450)

**Problem it solves:** "I don't actually know what's wrong."

Already a real deliverable. The Kemp & Kerrigan report, written 20 August 2026, found the
practice's own name returning a competitor group's booking page listing eleven other practices,
an NHS record published on 5 August with the wrong phone number, and nine directories still
running the previous owner's name. Every line date-stamped and URL-cited.

This is the strongest single asset in the business and it was being given away as a sales tool.
It stays a sales tool for prospecting. On the page it is priced, so the buyer knows what they got.

### 02 The Review Engine (£250)

**Problem it solves:** "I ask people to leave reviews but it's awkward and clearly not working."
(Verbatim, r/optometry, Sept 2025.)

Word-for-word scripts for the dispense desk, the collection call and the follow-up text. A
printed counter card carrying the direct review link, the one that opens the review box rather
than the listing. The prompt framework that gets a patient past the blank box and gets the
service and the town named in the review text, which is what actually moves the map pack.

Compliance is a selling point here, not a footnote. Everyone gets asked. Nobody gets filtered.
Review gating breaches Google's policy and the DMCCA, and the CMA enforces it.

### 03 The Recall Audit (£400)

**Problem it solves:** "There is revenue sitting in my own records and I can't see it."

Two years of records combed for patients overdue and never chased, plus the sequence that goes
to them. Evidence: a practice that ran this against 800 lapsed patients recovered 11% inside 90
days, roughly 88 appointments from one campaign.

This is the highest-ROI item in the entire research file and it costs Tom a morning.

### 04 The Front Desk Scripts (£350)

**Problem it solves:** "The phone rings, they ask how much an eye test is, and we lose them."

What to say when the enquiry is a price question, so the call ends in a booking rather than a
comparison. What to say at checkout to pre-appoint the next visit, repeatedly cited by
practitioners as the cheapest retention that exists. How to ask whether the partner and the
children are due too, which the same research calls the easiest referral available.

This is the "sales scripts" element and it is the one that most obviously pays for the retainer
on its own.

### 05 The Photography Visit (£550)

**Problem it solves:** "Every photo of my practice is a phone snap."

An hour on site. 25 to 30 edited images across People, Practice, Frames, Details and Exterior.
Full commercial licence, kept permanently regardless of what happens next.

### Held back for the call

Per Hormozi, do not spend the whole stack on the page. Two reserves, deployed against a
hesitating prospect:

- **The Review Response Playbook**: templates for the five star, the lukewarm three star and
  the angry one. Deploy when they raise a bad review they are still smarting about.
- **The Competitor Watch**: a monthly note on what the practice up the road is posting and
  ranking for. Deploy when they hesitate on price, because it makes the exclusivity concrete.

## 6. The guarantees

Three, layered, plus one deliberate refusal.

### Month one is refundable in full

£250, unconditional, no questions. They keep every photograph and every document. This is the
existing guarantee and it works. Worst case for the buyer is a free month of content and a
professional photo library.

### If it's late, it's free

New. Everything ships on the day it is due, every month, or that month costs nothing.

This is the guarantee that should scare Tom slightly, which per Hormozi is the test of a good
one. It is also entirely within his control, which makes it substantiable, and it answers the
single most common complaint about a marketing agency: they went quiet.

Do not extend this to results. A delivery promise can be kept. A patient-number promise cannot,
and under the CAP Code it would need substantiating before publication.

### No contract, no notice period

Monthly rolling. Cancel by text. The figure `0` carries this on the page.

### What is deliberately not guaranteed

A number of new patients. Saying so out loud converts better than promising one, because every
optician has already been promised one by somebody. The page states the refusal plainly and
then states what is measured instead.

## 7. Scarcity and urgency

Only use what is true.

**True and usable:** territorial exclusivity. When a town goes, it is gone for as long as that
practice stays. This is real supply constraint and it needs no dressing up.

**True and usable:** the price increase. £450 becomes £750. Anyone already quoted gets the old
rate if they start before a date Tom sets.

**Do not fabricate:** "three slots left this month". Tom's real monthly capacity is a number he
knows and I do not. If he wants a capacity line on the page, it must be his true figure. There
is a marked slot for it in `index.html` and it ships empty rather than invented.

Note the brand guideline: exclusivity is a closing lever, not an early headline claim. On the
page it therefore does not appear in the H1 or the hero. It appears at the pricing block, which
is exactly where a prospect hesitates, and it is framed as the reason the price is what it is.

---

## 8. Naming

The offer is deliberately not given a product name.

Every instinct says to call it The Local Visibility System or similar. Resist it. The entire
differentiation is that Tom is one person who turns up, not an agency with a methodology. A
branded system name makes him sound like the thing he is competing against, and it would
undercut the premium rather than support it.

The price is justified by the exclusivity, the person, and the stack. It does not need a name.

1. **Proof.** There is still no published case study and no client logo. Kemp & Kerrigan and
   Premier Eyecare have both been live since 1 August with baselines captured. The moment either

3. **Photography.** Every image slot on the page is marked. They must be real practice
   photographs, and the page argues for authentic photography while carrying none, which is the
   one place it currently contradicts itself.
4. **The AI question.** The FAQ answers "who writes this" honestly rather than claiming no AI is
   involved anywhere. That is an editorial call and Tom should confirm he is happy with it before
   it ships. My view is that it converts better than the alternative in 2026 and it is the only
   answer consistent with the house rules, which already mandate human review as the differentiator.
